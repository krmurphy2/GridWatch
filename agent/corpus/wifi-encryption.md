# Wi-Fi encryption and passwords

Your Wi-Fi encryption is the "lock" that stops strangers nearby from joining your
network or watching your traffic. The type of lock matters a lot.

## Which encryption modes are safe

- **WPA3** — the newest and strongest option. Use it if all your devices support it.
- **WPA2 (AES/CCMP)** — still strong and safe for everyday home use. A fine choice
  if some devices do not yet support WPA3.
- **WPA (original) / WEP / "Open" (no password)** — weak or broken. WEP can be
  cracked in minutes and "Open" means no protection at all. If your router is set
  to any of these, change it to WPA2 or WPA3.

Many routers offer a "WPA2/WPA3 mixed" or "transition" mode, which lets newer
devices use WPA3 while older ones fall back to WPA2. That is a reasonable setting
when you have a mix of old and new devices.

## Wi-Fi password tips

Use a long passphrase (a handful of random words is easy to type and hard to
guess). Your Wi-Fi password should be different from your router admin password.
You do not need to change your Wi-Fi password often — a strong one is fine to keep
unless you think it has been shared too widely.

## WPS

If your router has WPS (the push-button or PIN pairing feature), consider turning
it off. The PIN method in particular has known weaknesses.
